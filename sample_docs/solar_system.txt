The solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant molecular cloud. The Sun contains 99.86 percent of the system's mass. Jupiter is the largest planet and has a mass more than twice that of all the other planets combined. Saturn is famous for its extensive ring system, composed mostly of ice particles with a smaller amount of rocky debris. Mars has the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, which stands about 22 kilometers high. The asteroid belt lies between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. Neptune was the first planet located through mathematical prediction rather than direct observation.
